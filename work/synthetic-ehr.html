Matching distributions is only the beginning.

01 / TECHNICAL CHALLENGE

How do we evaluate synthetic data when matching individual distributions is not enough to preserve relationships and meaningful constraints?

02 / METHOD & PIPELINE

The pipeline prepares data in four related tables, generates synthetic records with Synthetic Data Vault, applies constraints to enforce referential integrity, and combines statistical checks with domain-specific validation rules. This makes structural and semantic failures visible separately.

  1. 01Prepare related tables
  2. 02Generate with SDV
  3. 03Enforce constraints
  4. 04Validate dependencies
03 / RESULTS & EVALUATION

A pilot of 10,000 synthetic patients achieved referential integrity, with no orphan keys among 37,753 condition records. Remaining age–diagnosis inconsistencies exposed dependencies that the tabular model had not captured.

Evaluation scope

This is a research pilot. Structural validity does not establish data utility or privacy protection; unresolved semantic errors remain part of the evaluation.

04 / MY CONTRIBUTION

I worked on the generation and validation pipeline and am first author of the OHDSI Europe 2026 contribution examining the gap between structural correctness and semantic coherence.

05 / APPLICATION & TRANSFERABLE SKILLS

The application is synthetic electronic health records in OMOP CDM: Person, Visit_Occurrence, Condition_Occurrence and Drug_Exposure. Clinical constraints provide a demanding test of whether generated records retain domain meaning.

What this work demonstrates

Relational data modelling, synthetic data generation, validation pipelines, constraint handling and model evaluation beyond aggregate metrics.
