CytoDiff

Improving classifiers when training data is scarce.

Parameter-efficient diffusion model fine-tuning, synthetic image generation and downstream evaluation to address limited, imbalanced training data.

01 / TECHNICAL CHALLENGE

Can synthetic images improve classifier performance when training examples are limited and class distributions are highly imbalanced?

02 / METHOD & PIPELINE

I fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1 with LoRA using few-shot examples, generated class-specific synthetic images, and measured their value through ResNet and CLIP classification. The pipeline connects training, generation and evaluation so that image quality is assessed alongside downstream performance.

  1. 01Few-shot examples
  2. 02LoRA fine-tuning
  3. 03Synthetic generation
  4. 04Classifier evaluation
03 / RESULTS & EVALUATION

Adding 5,000 synthetic images per class increased ResNet accuracy from 27% to 78% and CLIP accuracy from 62% to 77% in the reported limited-data experiment. I presented CytoDiff at the CVAMD workshop at ICCV 2025 in Honolulu.

Evaluation scope

These gains are specific to the study’s dataset and experimental setup. Generalisation to other datasets and settings requires further evaluation.

04 / MY CONTRIBUTION

I developed the training, generation and classification pipeline during my internship at Helmholtz Munich, under the supervision of Rao Muhammad Umer and Carsten Marr. The project became my bachelor’s thesis and an ICCV Workshops paper, on which I am first author.

05 / APPLICATION & TRANSFERABLE SKILLS

The study uses white blood cell images, where rare cell types create a challenging classification problem. My bioinformatics background helped connect model evaluation with the properties of the data.

What this work demonstrates

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning, computer vision, data augmentation, imbalanced learning and reproducible ML pipelines.
